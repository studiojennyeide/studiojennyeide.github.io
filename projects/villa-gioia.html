Villa Gioia

Grounds for Care: Thresholds between Villa and Garden

Year
2026
Type
Competition entry, Villa Gioia by TerraViva Competitions
Location
Bedizzole, Italy
Programme
Care residence
Team
Jenny Halvorsen Eide, Zeri Emilie Botani

The project reimagines the historic Villa Gioia and strengthens the space for care and quality of life. Architecture and landscape form one continuous gesture: a hierarchy of thresholds from the private room to the wintergarden, the courtyard and the garden. Each threshold offers a little more openness, company and movement. Along this route, walking, rest and meeting become part of the everyday, while the villa’s historic volumes and fabric are kept.

Program

The program follows the sequence of thresholds, from private to common and from quiet to active. Residences open onto the wintergarden, where residents can sit and pause; the common rooms along the courtyard, such as the dining area, gather them together; the garden offers space for body and mind. Throughout, seating, step-free access and daylight are part of every space, so each resident can choose how much activity and company they want.

Common areas accumulate on the ground floor, spilling out into the courtyard, which is also open to non-residents. Larger care and wellness programs make use of the bigger spans of the east wing, while the scattered interior spaces of the main palace become residences on the first and second floor. The west palace houses the staff residences.

The recreational programs are organized into four categories: gathering, rest & retreat, green infrastructure and sensory & wellbeing, strategically placed across the site to create a circulation between the different spaces. Each category is a balance of social interaction, relaxation, physical activity, green infrastructure, and health and wellbeing, establishing a strong sense of community among the residents. Everyday functions, including the dining area, vegetable garden, and lounge spaces, are located closer to the villa to ensure easy access and encourage daily use. Further into the garden, quieter recreational and sensory spaces offer opportunities for retreat, relaxation, and a deeper connection with nature.

Design Gestures

The main path winds through the garden and accesses all the spaces. Smaller paths act as shortcuts between the spaces while also offering alternative routes for longer, exploratory walks and a change of scenery. Taking the path inside, you access the first floor through the preserved staircase tower, or the inserted elevator for improved accessibility, and you access the rooms from passing through the wintergardens, opened to the courtyard.

In the wintergardens the sills of the windows facing the courtyard are lowered to let more light inside without disturbing the structural or historical rhythms of the facade. The lowered sills become a seat along the resident’s route from the stairs to the residences, and the tempered space becomes a social connector area.

The private rooms are fitted to comfortably accommodate 1-2 persons. A new entrance volume is carefully inserted, including an ensuite bathroom and wardrobe. Marked by a change of height and materials, it clearly separates the rooms into a living zone and a service zone, and provides a technical space above the bathroom. The inserted materials are light wood, and tile flooring, contrasting well with the existing structures.

The stone walls separating the grounds in the courtyard are removed to open up and connect the space to a whole. New ground is laid with flat cobblestones to secure easy accessibility between the buildings for the residents across times and seasons.

The existing trees are preserved, while carefully placed elements fill the gaps and reinforce the historic tree line on the site. The terrain is lightly modified close to the amphitheater, to ensure better accessibility and make the area less steep. The new trees planted south-east on the site will shield the residents as they are using the garden, from the noise and view of the road.

Throughout the project, sensitive implementations and reinterpretations of the historic fabric form the grounds for care and wellbeing in Villa Gioia, allowing the site’s heritage to inform the design language without compromising its existing character.
